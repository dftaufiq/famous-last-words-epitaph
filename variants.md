# Variant selection — Famous Last Words: The Funniest Epitaph

10 variants drafted, all first-person dead voice, all ≤150 chars, all a
single line of dying speech that reads like carved stone. Each tested:
"di mana twist-nya?" — no twist, discard. Pairwise judging used the judge's
own lexicographic criteria from RULES.txt (1 on-subject epitaph of famous
last words, 2 real laugh from a genuine joke, 3 surprise/twist/double
meaning, 4 carved-in-stone voice, 5 quotable). Margin rule (§10f): pick the
variant that wins DECISIVELY, not thinly.

## The 10 variants (with char counts)

- **A** (84): "My last words were 'hold my beer.' This stone says 'beloved
  husband.' Both are lies."
- **B** (100): "I died doing what I loved: ignoring the check-engine light
  for 14 months. The car is fine. I am not."
- **C** (102): "The stone says I died peacefully. I died arguing about the
  thermostat. It was 68 degrees. I was right."
- **D** (107): "I told my wife I'd fix the roof when I got around to it.
  I'm dead now. The roof still leaks. She was right."
- **E** (115): "I bet Dave $50 I could hold my breath until the ambulance
  arrived. Dave, you owe me $50. Collect it from the stone."
- **F** (97): "My dying wish was a quiet funeral. My family hired a DJ. I
  can hear him from here. He's terrible."
- **G** (98): "They spelled my name wrong on the stone. I've been dead 12
  years and this is still the worst part."
- **H** (107): "I asked for my ashes to be scattered at the beach. My
  family used a leaf blower. I'm in three counties now."
- **I** (131): "Here lies Bob. He asked for 'beloved father' on the stone.
  The stonecutter charged extra for honesty. This is what we could afford."
- **J** (88): "I left everything to the dog. He was the only one who never
  asked me to fix his printer."

## Discards before the tournament

- **J — DISCARDED (weakest punch):** the dog/printer setup is charming but
  the punch is a mild observation, not a turn — loses criterion 2 to any
  variant with a real twist. Also office-adjacent in a field that already
  has "Do Not Resuscitate. Do Not Reply All."
- **G — DISCARDED (thin twist):** a typo complaint is funny but the punch
  ("this is still the worst part") states the joke instead of landing it —
  loses criterion 3; no second beat.
- **D — DISCARDED at semifinal (familiar trope):** "She was right" is a
  well-worn domestic punch; the roof setup is good but the final beat is
  one the judge has read a thousand times. Loses criterion 5 (freshness).
- **F — DISCARDED at semifinal (thin final beat):** "He's terrible" is a
  good kicker but the DJ setup is one beat; no escalation, no specific
  number, no named character. Loses to variants with a second beat.

## Pairwise tournament (6 survivors: A, B, C, E, H, I)

- C vs H: H wins narrowly — "I'm in three counties now" is a more visual,
  more surprising punch than "I was right"; C's correction-of-the-stone
  family also has a near neighbor in the field ("The Autopsy Disagreed").
- E vs I: E wins narrowly — "Collect it from the stone" is a sharper final
  beat than "This is what we could afford"; I's setup takes 131 chars to
  land, E lands in 115 with a named character and a specific amount.
- A vs C: A wins — "Both are lies" double-negates the epitaph itself, a
  stronger twist than C's single correction; A is also the most on-subject
  (it quotes the last words AND the stone).
- B vs H: B wins DECISIVELY — B's reversal ("The car is fine. I am not.")
  lands in four words with zero parsing; H's leaf-blower image is funny
  but needs the reader to picture the scene, and "three counties" is a
  gentler payoff.
- A vs E: A wins narrowly — both have strong final beats, but A's
  self-negating epitaph is tighter (84 chars) and more on-brief (criterion
  1: it IS the last words and the stone, arguing with each other); E's bet
  premise is slightly convoluted (why bet on holding your breath until an
  ambulance arrives?).
- B vs A (final): B wins DECISIVELY — A needs a beat of parsing ("both are
  lies" requires resolving two claims against each other); B lands instantly
  on one read, and the judge brief says a line that needs an explanation to
  land has not landed. B's angle is also fresher: A's dare family is
  crowded in the field ("I said 'watch this.' Now I'm the warning."), while
  NO public entry does cars or the check-engine light. B's final beat is
  the most quotable of all 10 lines.

## Winner: B

> I died doing what I loved: ignoring the check-engine light for 14 months. The car is fine. I am not.

- 100 chars / 100 bytes. Why it wins TELAK, not tipis:
  1. On-subject from word one ("I died doing what I loved" — a classic
     epitaph opening, subverted into mundane neglect).
  2. Real twist: the beloved activity is ignoring a warning light; the
     punch reverses the expected outcome (the car outlived the driver).
  3. Escalation with a specific number (14 months) and a deadpan voice.
  4. Punch at the very end, four words: "The car is fine. I am not." —
     a clean reversal, quotable on its own.
  5. Fresh: no public entry does cars/check-engine; avoids all crowded
     angles (AI/context, password, dare, support hold, subscriptions,
     snooze, office).
- Backup if resubmission is ever needed (blind-round rule): A (the
  self-negating epitaph, "Both are lies") is the strongest alternate;
  E ("Collect it from the stone") second.
